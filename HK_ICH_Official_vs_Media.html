A Digital Analysis of Cultural Narratives: Comparing Official and Media Representations of Hong Kong Intangible Cultural Heritage

Research question: Are there any differences between the official descriptions and the media about traditional craftsmanship and festive events?

Scope: six items on the national list of intangible cultural heritage (ICH) in Hong Kong · Official source: Hong Kong ICH Database (hkichdb.gov.hk) · Media source: news and web articles supplied in the uploaded TXT file. DIFH2005 Digital Humanities Methodologies project.

Data and methodology

Documented so that each decision can be inspected (cf. cleaning and tokenisation as acts of judgement).

1. Identifying the six items and the texts

Official_All.txt has one labelled paragraph per item (=== item ===), so six official texts. The media file has formal headers (=== item | outlet | date ===) for only 11 articles (Tin Hau 5, Cheung Chau Jiao 5, Tai O 1). After the last header, text for Tai O, Tai Hang, Yu Lan and Wong Tai Sin follows without headers, mixed under one block. I therefore assigned them using in-text markers (numbered headings such as “2.”, “Source:” lines, outlet names and the subject of the text). This yields media articles. These boundaries for 18 articles are my inference, not given labels; the exact line ranges are listed below. One block headed “Tai O” (“4. Immerse yourself in the captivating Tai Hang Fire Dragon Dance”) is actually about Tai Hang and was reassigned.

2. Cleaning

Removed: ferry/timetable bullet lines, “Source:”, “Date:”, “Time:”, “Location:” lines, timestamps, URLs, exact duplicate lines and numerals. Chinese characters ( in the media file; none in the Official file) were removed because they occur only as glosses or in one mixed-language article; with so little Chinese text, a separate Chinese word-segmentation analysis would not be reliable, so this study analyses English only. Text was lower-cased and punctuation removed.

3. Tokenisation

Multi-word names are merged into one token (e.g. tin hau, tai hang, wong tai sin, chiu chow, fire dragon, dragon boat, sai kung). Words under 3 letters and standard English stopwords are removed, plus corpus-wide filler (hong, kong, month and weekday names, “said”, “may”, etc.). Simple plural folding (temples→temple) applies only where the singular exists in the corpus. No stemming beyond that. Frequencies are reported both as raw counts and per 1,000 meaningful tokens, because the datasets differ greatly in size.

4. Statistics

Emphasis uses Dunning’s log-likelihood (G²) on the two frequencies given each corpus size. G² ≥ 3.84 is conventionally significant at p<0.05, but it is only a guide, especially with a very small Official corpus. A word is called exclusive only if its count in the other source is exactly 0; otherwise it is merely more prominent.

5. Co-occurrence networks

Window = one sentence. For each source, the 48 most frequent words are candidates; each pair of different words appearing in the same sentence adds 1 to the link weight. Links are shown if weight ≥3 (Official) or ≥12 (Media); the thresholds differ because the corpora differ in size by ~18×. Clusters come from greedy modularity maximisation (NetworkX). Node size = word frequency, link width = co-occurrence count.

6. Theme lexicons

Themes are my analytical categories, not findings. Each is a transparent word list (shown beneath the chart) matched to the corpus vocabulary; counts are per 1,000 tokens.
